# Research Pack — Kodak and the Digital Camera

## Core verified story
- Kodak engineer Steven Sasson built the first self-contained digital camera prototype in 1975.
- The prototype was experimental: roughly 0.01 megapixel, recorded black-and-white images to a cassette, and took about 23 seconds to capture an image.
- Kodak did not simply 'hide one finished product and die.' The more accurate story is that Kodak participated in digital imaging for years, but its economics and organizational incentives remained heavily tied to film, photo processing, and printing.
- Kodak filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection in January 2012 after the photography market had shifted dramatically toward digital imaging.

## Editorial angle
Avoid the viral oversimplification: 'Kodak invented digital and refused to use it.'
Use the stronger lesson: a company can recognize a technological change and still fail to redesign its business model fast enough around that change.

## Fact-safety rules
- Do not claim one executive personally killed digital photography unless a primary source supports it.
- Do not imply the 1975 prototype was consumer-ready.
- Separate invention of the prototype from later commercialization of digital cameras.

## Suggested primary/authoritative source targets for final evidence traceability
- U.S. National Inventors Hall of Fame profile for Steven Sasson.
- Kodak historical/company materials on digital imaging where available.
- Kodak Chapter 11 announcement / SEC-era corporate records.
