# Research Pack 001 — Netflix / Blockbuster

## Core claim

In 2000, Netflix founders Reed Hastings and Marc Randolph met Blockbuster leadership and proposed a deal valuing Netflix at about $50 million, with Netflix positioned to run the online side. Blockbuster declined.

## Source notes

1. Marc Randolph first-person account, Built In (2022): https://builtin.com/articles/netflix-blockbuster-buyout
2. TechCrunch history feature (2023): https://techcrunch.com/2023/04/22/netflix-history-25-years/
3. Fortune retrospective (2026): https://fortune.com/2026/01/10/netflix-warner-bros-paramount-acquisistion-blockbuster-reed-hastings-marc-randolph/

## Safe phrasing

Use: "Netflix proposed a deal valuing the company at roughly $50 million; Blockbuster declined."

Avoid exaggerated claims such as "Blockbuster could have owned today's entire Netflix for exactly $50 million with certainty" because the proposed structure and later outcome require context.

## Story angle

The lesson is not simply that Blockbuster was foolish. Netflix was loss-making and the dot-com market was collapsing. The stronger story is how reasonable decisions can look very different when technology changes the economics of an industry.
